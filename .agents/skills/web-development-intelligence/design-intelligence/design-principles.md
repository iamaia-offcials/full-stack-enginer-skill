# Core Design Principles & Visual Mechanics

Master the psychological and perceptual rules that distinguish amateur templates from professional, high-craft web design.

## Fundamental Perceptual Laws

1. **Gestalt Principles in Interface Layout:**
   - **Proximity:** Elements placed close together are perceived as belonging to the same functional group. Related elements (label + input + help text) must have smaller gaps between them (4–8px) than the gap separating unrelated groups (24–32px).
   - **Similarity:** Elements sharing visual properties (color, shape, typography) are perceived as sharing a semantic role. Use identical styling for buttons with identical consequences.
   - **Continuity & Alignment:** The human eye follows linear vectors. Maintain crisp horizontal and vertical alignment baselines; rag edges in multi-column cards create visual chaos.
   - **Common Region & Closure:** A subtle background container or border cleanly encapsulates related items without needing heavy card borders.

2. **Optical Balance vs Mathematical Centering:**
   - **The Play Button Trap:** An equilateral triangle inside a circle must be shifted slightly right of mathematical center (approx. +8% offset) to appear optically centered.
   - **Cap-Height Alignment:** Icons placed next to text should align to the font's cap-height, not the total bounding line-box.
   - **Visual Weight Compensation:** Round shapes (circles, organic blobs) must be sized 4–6% larger in bounding box dimensions than adjacent hard-edged squares to appear equal in visual mass.

3. **Whitespace as Active Architecture:**
   - Whitespace is not empty space; it is the structural scaffolding of attention.
   - Micro-whitespace (between characters, words, list items) determines readability.
   - Macro-whitespace (between sections, columns, hero elements) determines perceived luxury, authority, and calm. Low-cost retail uses dense layouts; premium luxury brands use expansive macro-whitespace.
