# Information Architecture & User Flow Design

Structure digital spaces so users locate information intuitively and navigate with minimal cognitive friction.

## Principles of Navigation & Sitemaps

1. **Navigation Hierarchy Models:**
   - **Flat Navigation (0–7 Top-level Items):** Optimal for most marketing and commercial sites. Prevents deep nesting where critical content gets buried.
   - **Hub & Spoke:** Central dashboard or index linking out to specialized tasks (e.g. account settings, order history, inventory).
   - **Faceted Navigation:** For large catalogs (ecommerce, real estate). Multi-dimensional filtering (category, price, size, availability) with stable URL parameters.

2. **Reading Patterns & Eye Scanning:**
   - **F-Pattern (Content-Heavy):** Users scan horizontal headline, drop down to scan a second shorter line, then skim vertically down the left edge. Place key anchors and primary keywords along the left rail.
   - **Z-Pattern (Landing Pages / Presentation):** Eye moves from top-left (Logo) to top-right (Primary Action), sweeps diagonally across the hero visual, and settles on bottom-left/bottom-right CTA.
   - **Layer-Cake Pattern:** Scanners read only headings and subheadings. If headings do not tell a coherent story on their own, the page fails scannability.

3. **Content Priority Sequencing (The Inverted Pyramid):**
   - **Top 20% Viewport:** What is this? Why does it matter to me? What can I do right now?
   - **Middle 50%:** Concrete evidence, how it works, feature breakdowns, social proof, and comparison.
   - **Bottom 30%:** Risk reversal (FAQ, guarantees), secondary conversion, and navigational footer.
