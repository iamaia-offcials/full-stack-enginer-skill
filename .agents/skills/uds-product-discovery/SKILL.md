# Product discovery

## Inputs
Idea, audience, region/market, budget/resource constraints, and available interviews, analytics, or competitors. Ordinary code fixes do not require discovery.

## Procedure
1. Define the user problem, desired outcome, and research scope. Separate facts from assumptions.
2. Identify segments, jobs to be done, current alternatives, and usage situations.
3. When current market facts are needed, consult official/current evidence and record sources, dates, and uncertainty. Do not invent metrics or market size.
4. Compare alternatives by user needs rather than feature counts; show meaningful differences and limits.
5. List hypotheses and inexpensive validation methods such as interviews, prototypes, or analytics. Assess unvalidated-demand risk.
6. Carry only verified facts or explicitly labeled hypotheses into requirements.

## Output and verification
Provide a research brief with segments, jobs, alternatives, sourced/dated facts, hypotheses, prioritized experiments, and unresolved questions. Hypotheses are not proven facts; do not promise sales outcomes.
